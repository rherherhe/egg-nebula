Seasonal Skies and a Tale between Orion and Scorpius]
The Earth revolves around the Sun and depending on its position relative to the Sun, we can see different sets of stars at night.
This change is gradual, as stars seem to rise and set four minutes earlier each night. After 30 days, these same stars would have set 120 minutes (2 hours) earlier! And after a year, we are back to seeing the same set of stars (2 hours/month × 12 months = 24 hours).
You might have noticed that you mostly see Orion during chilly December nights. Six months after, late June, the Earth would have completed half of its orbit and we will be located on the other side of the Sun. During months like this, we can no longer see Orion because it is now located on the same direction as the Sun. Instead, we are graced with another beautiful constellation, Scorpius.
🌟🌌The Tale of Orion and Scorpius🌌🌟
Orion was a great hunter. One day, he boasted to the goddess Artemis that there's no animal on Earth he will not be able to kill. This made mother Earth (Gaia) furious she sent a scorpion (Scorpius) to kill Orion.
Scorpius and Orion had a great battle resulting to Orion's death. Their battle was able to catch the attention of Zeus and the god honored Scorpius by putting him in the sky. Artemis requested Zeus to honor Orion as well, and Zeus obliged and put Orion at the opposite end of the sky. To this day, we can still see Scorpius endlessly chasing Orion at the sky.
