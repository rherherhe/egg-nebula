Getting to Know the Night Sky Part 1

You can see many wonderful things with just your naked eyes, you just have to know where and when to look 🙂

1. The Moon - the Moon rises and sets at different times of the day depending on which phase it is currently on. Full Moon rises at sunset and sets at sunrise, while the New Moon rises and sets at the same time with the Sun making it hard to see.

2. Stars, Constellations, Asterisms - the sky is divided into 88 regions called constellations. These constellations typically represent animals, mythological characters, or man-made objects. Asterisms are patterns in the sky which may or may not be a part of a constellation. An example of a popular asterism is the Big Dipper which is part of the constellation Ursa Major.

3. Planets - Five planets are visible in the night sky with the naked eye. They appear like bright stars which do not twinkle. They are found in the ecliptic, which is the apparent path that the Sun follows over the course of a year.

4. Nebulae - A nebula is an interstellar cloud of gas and dust where stars form. One example of a naked eye nebula is the Orion's Nebula which is the middle 'star' in Orion's sword.

5. Meteors, Fireballs, Bolides - a meteor is a streak of light that forms when a meteorite enters the Earth's atmosphere. Fireballs are very bright meteors, while a Bolide is a fireball that explodes in the atmosphere.

6. Comets - a comet is an icy Solar System body which orbits the Sun. When it gets close to the Sun, it heats up and produces a visible atmosphere called coma, and sometimes a tail.

7. Star Clusters - star clusters are group of stars that are gravitationally bound. There are two types of star clusters-- open clusters which are mainly composed of young stars, and globular clusters which are composed of older stars. An example of a star cluster that is visible in the naked eyes is Pleiades (aka The Rosary, or Seven Sisters) which is found on the constellation Taurus.

8. Galaxies - Galaxies were originally called as spiral nebulae and were thought to be just part of the Milky Way Galaxy. Now we know that the Milky Way is just one of the billions of galaxies in the Universe.

9. Milky Way - We live in the edge of the Orion spiral arm of the Milky Way so we can see its center from the Earth on a dark sky site. For those who live in the Northern Hemisphere, it is visible from March to October, and is best viewed from April to July.

10. Large and Small Magellanic Clouds - the magellanic clouds are two dwarf galaxies orbiting the Milky Way. They are visible on the Southern Hemisphere.

11. Artificial satellites - You can see the international space station (ISS) and other satellites from Earth! They look like moving stars and can only be seen shortly after sunrise and before sunset.
